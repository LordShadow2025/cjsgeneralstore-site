Growing Up GenX

The Stove Knocked Back

By C.B. Barkley · October 11, 2026 · 3 min read

Every GenX kid had a house story. The one where something didn't add up, where the grown-ups had an explanation and you didn't believe a word of it. This is mine, and I still don't have an explanation.

The house

It was Tennessee, in the early 80s, and I was little. My family rented an old two-story house with an attic. Rentals back then came the way they came: creaky floors, mismatched wallpaper, doors that stuck in the summer, and nobody ever told you who lived there before. My sister claimed the attic as her room, because of course she did. An attic room is the coolest room in any house when you're a kid, right up until the lights go out.

There was also a side door in that house that we never went into. I can't tell you why. Nobody made a rule about it. We just didn't. Every family has a door like that, and every kid in that family knows exactly where it is without ever talking about it.

The knock

I was small enough that my sister could still carry me around, and one day she was doing just that, the two of us goofing off the way siblings do. We ended up by the stove, and I reached out and knocked on it, the way you'd knock on a door.

It knocked back.

Not a pop from the oven cooling down, not a pipe settling somewhere in the wall. A knock. Right back at us, like someone on the other side was answering. I don't remember what my sister said. I remember both of us going very quiet, and I remember getting out of that kitchen a lot faster than we came in.

The spider

Some time later, I woke up to a giant spider. Not a little house spider up in the corner of the ceiling, either. It was right beside my head, close enough that it was the first thing I saw when I opened my eyes. That's the kind of moment that makes you hold your breath and stop moving entirely while you try to figure out what to do next. Maybe it was just a spider. Tennessee has plenty of them, and so do old houses. But in that house, it didn't feel like just anything.

The stairs

And then there were the shadows. I saw them walking down the stairs. Shapes moving the way people move, coming down from the floor above, with nobody there to cast them. You learn a lot about yourself as a kid when you see something like that. Mostly you learn how still you can lie under a blanket and how long you can go without blinking.

Back then, nobody had a phone to grab a picture with, and nobody would have believed a kid anyway. You told your sister, maybe a friend at school, and you carried it around with you. That's how a lot of GenX ghost stories survived: passed between kids, never quite proven, never quite forgotten.

Going back

Years later, I went looking for that house. I still remembered the street address, so I typed it into Google, half expecting to see that same old two-story staring back at me. I wanted to see it again as a grown-up and decide for myself whether it was just an old rental with bad pipes and a kid's imagination.

What came up was an empty lot. No house, no attic, no side door. Just grass where all of it used to be.

I don't know what happened to the house, or to that side door, or to whatever was on the other side of the stove. Maybe that's for the best. Some places are better left as memories. But every now and then, when I knock on something without thinking, part of me still waits to hear if anything knocks back.

Your turn

Did you grow up in a house like that? A room nobody used, a sound nobody could explain, a door you just didn't open? GenX kids collected stories like these the way we collected baseball cards and stickers. If this one sounded familiar, you might like the strange goings-on in the Unfound Library, or a quieter trip back with Saturday Morning.
