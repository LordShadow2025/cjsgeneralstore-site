Saturday Morning: The Best Two Hours of the Week

By C.B. Barkley · October 11, 2026 · 3 min read

There was one morning a week when GenX kids got up early without being told to. No alarm, no school bus, no arguing. Just pajamas, a bowl of cereal, and the television, all to ourselves, before the grown-ups were awake.

Appointment television, before we knew the phrase

If you missed a show on Saturday morning, you missed it. There was no recording it, no streaming it later, no clip online. The cartoons came on when they came on, and the whole neighborhood was watching the same thing at the same time. On Monday at school, everyone had seen the same episodes. That's a kind of shared experience that's hard to come by now.

Saturday morning was also the only time cartoons truly owned the networks. For a few hours, the big channels put away the news and the grown-up shows and handed the whole lineup over to kids.

Fighting the television first

Getting the cartoons on was its own little adventure. You might have had to turn the big dial on the set, nudge the rabbit-ear antenna until the picture stopped rolling, or hold the antenna in just the right spot while your brother watched. Some mornings you got up so early that the station was still running the test pattern, so you sat there with your cereal and waited. The volume stayed low, because waking up Mom and Dad meant the TV might get turned off.

The lineup

Everybody remembers their own favorites, but some shows were everywhere. The Smurfs ran on NBC through most of the 80s, and you either loved Smurfette or had a cousin who did. Muppet Babies took the Muppets back to the nursery and turned imagination into an adventure every week. Pee-wee's Playhouse arrived in 1986 and was unlike anything else on television, with talking furniture, a secret word, and a whole lot of screaming.

And between the shows, there was Schoolhouse Rock. Ask any GenX'er how a bill becomes a law, or what a conjunction does, and there's a decent chance they'll sing you the answer.

The cereal

Saturday morning had its own menu. It was the one day sugar cereal felt fully allowed, poured into the biggest bowl in the house and eaten on the floor a little too close to the screen. Half the commercials were for cereal anyway, and the other half were for toys, usually toys from the same shows you were watching. Nobody thought twice about it. We just wanted the prize at the bottom of the box.

The slow fade

By late morning the lineup started to change. The cartoons gave way to music shows, sports, or old movies, and that was your signal to get dressed and go outside. The best part of the day was already over, but there was a whole Saturday left, and you'd be back at it next week.

Over the years, cable channels, home video, and eventually streaming made cartoons available any time, which is great, and also why Saturday morning slowly disappeared. You can watch anything now. But there's something about waiting all week for it that made it taste better.

Keep it going

What was your Saturday morning lineup? Which cereal did you fight your siblings for? Pull up a seat in the Clubhouse, where the GenX Saturday Morning channel runs a block of those old favorites, and the Bob Ross and MST3K streams are always on.
